# Battery habits will decide this Air's lifespan

This MacBook Air (Mac17,3, M5, 16 GB, 512 GB) is healthy in every subsystem measured, and the only part ageing on a visible clock is the battery. The SSD has used roughly **0.8–4 % of its likely endurance** (12.08 TB written against an estimated 300–1,500 TB budget for a 512 GB Apple drive), with **0 media errors and 100 % spare**. Memory is fine (**0.9 GB swap** sits inside the range Apple-focused researchers call unworrying), and **231 GB free of 460 GB (about 50 %)** is about double the free-space level experts recommend. The battery reads **97 % "Maximum Capacity" after 97 of its 1,000 rated cycles**, but the gauge's own nominal capacity is lower at **93.2 % of design**. Early capacity loss is expected to be front-loaded, so this is not yet alarming. It is still the number Talon should watch most closely. Heat is a performance story on this fanless machine, not a lifespan one. Sustained all-core work loses about **14 % of multi-core speed** after 30 minutes, and there is no evidence that the high die temperatures Apple's firmware allows damage the chip. Batteries are different: heat and long stretches at 100 % charge are the documented causes of permanent capacity loss. So Talon should be built around three jobs: keeping the battery off 100 % and out of heat, catching the rare SSD red flags (media errors, spare depletion), and explaining throttling honestly rather than alarming on raw temperatures.

Throughout this report, statements with a citation are sourced facts. Anything labelled **Proposal** is a Talon design choice derived from those facts, and anything labelled **Unverified** has a weak or single source. Measured values come from the user's own machine.

## A 10-core, 3 nm machine with a soldered 512 GB drive

The M5 is Apple's third-generation 3 nm SoC. It has **4 "super" cores plus 6 efficiency cores**, up to 10 GPU cores with a Neural Accelerator in each, a 16-core Neural Engine, and **153 GB/s memory bandwidth, nearly 30 % more than the M4** ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/10/apple-unleashes-m5-the-next-big-leap-in-ai-performance-for-apple-silicon/)). "Super core" is the new name for what Apple used to call performance cores. Apple applied it to all M5 chips in March 2026 and reused "performance" for a new mid-tier core in the M5 Pro and Max ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/20/apple-explains-why-m5-chips-have-three-different-core-types-in-new-interview/)). Mac17,3 is the 13-inch model (A3449), announced 3 March 2026 and shipped 11 March 2026 ([The Apple Wiki](https://theapplewiki.com/wiki/Mac17,3); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/03/03/apple-announces-macbook-air-with-m5/)). The user's on-device DVFS tables top out at **4,464 MHz on the super cores, 3,048 MHz on the efficiency cores and 1,578 MHz on the GPU**. Notebookcheck's spec table rounds the super-core figure to 4.6 GHz ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Insane-performance-and-efficiency-without-fans-Apple-MacBook-Air-13-M5-Entry-Review.1242707.0.html)), so Talon should read the maximum from the machine rather than hard-code it.

The SSD is the same **APPLE SSD AP0512Z, a PCIe 4.0 part** that Notebookcheck measured at **6,473 MB/s read and 6,559 MB/s write**, against 2,833 / 1,988 MB/s for the M4 Air's drive ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Insane-performance-and-efficiency-without-fans-Apple-MacBook-Air-13-M5-Entry-Review.1242707.0.html)). Notebookcheck's review unit had 16 GB of LPDDR5x-9600 unified memory. Early reports say both RAM and SSD are soldered, so a failure of either means a logic-board replacement ([BornCity](https://borncity.com/news/apple-macbook-air-m5-mehr-leistung-alte-reparaturprobleme/)). That is **Unverified** for the M5 Air specifically: no teardown was found, although Apple has soldered both parts since at least the 2020 Air ([iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/mac/ifixit-macbook-air-2020-teardown)).

The battery is a **~53.8 Wh lithium-polymer pack** ([The Apple Wiki](https://theapplewiki.com/wiki/Mac17,3); [Connection.com](https://www.connection.com/product/apple-macbook-air-13-m5-chip-w-10c-cpu-and-10c-gpu-16gb-1tb-ssd-starlight/mdhc4ll-a/42114533)). Apple rates it for **1,000 cycles to retain up to 80 % of original capacity** ([Apple Support 102888](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102888)). An iFixit-derived listing gives 52.6 Wh instead ([iFixit mirror](https://portalidea.wiki/ifixit_pt_all_2026-03/Device/MacBook_Air_13%22_2026_%28M5%29)). The user's own numbers settle the question in favour of 53.8 Wh: a DesignCapacity of 4,629 mAh times about 11.62 V nominal for three cells in series gives 53.8 Wh. The battery exposes three per-cell Qmax values, which confirms the 3-cell series pack. On sensors, the machine's HID list exposes about 39 temperature channels: "PMU tdie1–14", "PMU tdev2–8", "PMU2 …", "PMU tcal", "NAND CH0 temp" and "gas gauge battery". The widely used Stats app maps these to power-management-unit die, power-management-unit device, disk and battery temperatures ([Stats values.swift](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exelban/stats/master/Modules/Sensors/values.swift)). Apple documents none of them.

## Fanless design costs about 14 % of sustained speed, not lifespan

The 13-inch M5 Air can draw up to about **24 W of CPU power in short bursts and 9 W sustained**, with a GPU budget of 16.5 W ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Insane-performance-and-efficiency-without-fans-Apple-MacBook-Air-13-M5-Entry-Review.1242707.0.html)). Under combined CPU and GPU stress, package power peaks at about 26 W and then settles near 8 W. A CPU-only Cinebench run drops to about 13 W within a couple of minutes. After 30 minutes, the Cinebench 2024 multi-core score falls from **957 to 820 at 9 W, about 14 % lower**, and the GPU loses about 18 % in a sustained Steel Nomad Light run. The chassis peaks at **44.1 °C on top and 42.4 °C underneath** ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Insane-performance-and-efficiency-without-fans-Apple-MacBook-Air-13-M5-Entry-Review.1242707.0.html)). The fan-cooled M5 MacBook Pro 14 sustains about 15 W on the CPU and runs about 40 % faster in long gaming loads ([Notebookcheck news](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-MacBook-Air-13-M5-gasps-for-air-as-MacBook-Pro-14-M5-shows-40-higher-gaming-performance-in-Cyberpunk-2077.1264788.0.html)). One consumer guide claims about 40 % CPU throttling ([Evetech](https://evezone.evetech.co.za/deep-dives/is-the-base-m5-mac-enough-what-the-fanless-air-s-40-throttle-means-for-you)), but no other source supports that figure and it should be disregarded. Single-core speed and short bursts are unaffected, and Notebookcheck saw no slowdown on battery power.

The user measured the die going from 38 to 50 °C in six seconds at full clocks. That fits a design that spends its thermal mass first and cuts power only later. Apple publishes no maximum junction temperature (tjmax). In community tests, an M3 15-inch Air peaked at **114 °C and then held about 100 °C** by throttling ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/apple-m3-macbook-air-hits-114-degrees-celsius-under-full-load.284829)), and the M5 MacBook Pro averaged about **99 °C** in Cinebench ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2951397/the-m5-macbook-pro-runs-hotter-than-the-m4-it-replaces.html)). No die-temperature trace has been published for the M5 Air. No source shows that sustained operation inside Apple's control envelope shortens the SoC's life. Intel's analogous position is that running near the junction limit "isn't necessarily cause for concern" ([Intel](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000005597/processors.html)).

The authoritative throttling signal is macOS's five-level thermal pressure state: **nominal, moderate, heavy, trapping, sleeping**. `thermald` publishes it through the Darwin notification `com.apple.system.thermalpressurelevel`, which needs no root. The public `ProcessInfo.thermalState` reports both moderate and heavy as "fair" ([stanislas.blog](https://stanislas.blog/2025/12/macos-thermal-throttling-app/)). The same author found that one undocumented IOKit temperature read topped out around 80 °C while iStat Menus showed over 100 °C on the same Mac. Sensor choice changes the headline number dramatically.

**Proposal:** Talon should lead with thermal pressure, not degrees. It should report "throttling" only when CPU busy is near 100 %, super-core residency sits below 4,464 MHz, and package power is falling, all at once. For the temperature display, use the maximum of PMU tdie1–14 as the "SoC hotspot", and leave out tcal, which on an M2 stayed fixed at 51 °C and is likely a calibration constant ([MacRumors forum](https://spy.macrumors.com/threads/temps-on-new-m2-macbook-pros.2378659)). Reading the M5 SMC keys Tp00/Tp04/Tp08/Tp0C gives true per-super-core temperatures, but that key list comes from the community and must be checked on the device.

## The battery is the one component on a visible clock

Lithium-ion cells age through two mechanisms. **Calendar ageing** is driven by time spent at high charge and high temperature. **Cycle ageing** is driven by depth of discharge, charge rate and temperature while charging. For a laptop that sits plugged in, the controllable factors are time at 100 % and heat. Battery University's generic-cell storage data shows the size of the effect. After one year at 25 °C, a cell held at 40 % charge keeps **96 %** of its capacity, while one held at 100 % keeps **80 %**. At 40 °C the figures are 85 % and 65 % ([Battery University BU-808](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-808-how-to-prolong-lithium-based-batteries)). Research from KIT found that charge level matters little below about 80 % and a great deal at 100 % ([KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000133836/117458694)). A 13-year dataset of commercial NMC cells showed calendar fade following a **square-root-of-time law**, which means a new pack loses capacity fastest in its first months ([OSTI 2536694](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2536694)). Shallow cycles are much gentler than full ones: Battery University lists about 300 cycles to 70 % capacity at 100 % depth of discharge, against about 1,000 cycles at 40 % depth ([BU-808](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-808-how-to-prolong-lithium-based-batteries)). These are lab figures for generic cells. They show the direction and rough size of the effect, but they do not predict this pack.

Apple's own guidance agrees. The Mac works best at **10–35 °C ambient**. Ambient temperatures above 35 °C "can permanently damage battery capacity", and charging in heat does further damage. For long-term storage, Apple advises about 50 % charge, recharged to 50 % every six months ([Apple Batteries](https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/)). Since **macOS Tahoe 26.4**, Apple silicon Macs have had a built-in **Charge Limit, adjustable from 80 to 100 %**, in System Settings > Battery > (i) next to Charging. It sits alongside the older machine-learning-based Optimized Battery Charging. Both features occasionally charge to 100 % on purpose to recalibrate the state-of-charge estimate ([Apple Support 102338](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102338)). On macOS 27.0.1, users have reported a gauge-calibration routine (`ChargingUpForGauging`) that never completes and overrides both the native limit and AlDente ([AlDente #1842](https://github.com/AppHouseKitchen/AlDente-Battery_Care_and_Monitoring/issues/1842)). The affected machines were M1 Pro/Max and M3 Macs, and several had third-party replacement batteries. One Apple Community post reports an M5 Air ignoring its 80 % limit ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256286525)). Both reports are **Unverified** for this machine. Third-party limiters have been badly disrupted on macOS 27: the SMC keys they used to write are missing or locked, and AlDente and batt now mostly drive Apple's own limit through private paths ([batt README](https://github.com/charlie0129/batt); [macos-tahoe.com](https://macos-tahoe.com/blog/macos-27-battery-charge-limit-apps-broken-aldente-batt-charging-to-100-fix-2026/)). **Proposal:** Talon should never write SMC keys itself. It should recommend the native Charge Limit and only read state.

| Swap | 0.9 GB | Occasional 1–2 GB is fine; regular 4 GB+ is a concern | Healthy |
| Free space | 231 of 460 GB (about 50 %) | At least 60 GB, ideally 15–25 % free | Very healthy |

The battery gap needs explaining. Apple does not publish its "Maximum Capacity" formula. Apple Community users report that computing it from raw values gives a number 2–5 % off the displayed figure ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256021109)). On this machine, 97 % lines up best with average per-cell Qmax ÷ design (about 97.9 %, rounded down). That is an **Unverified** inference from a single data point, and it does not match the formula the batt maintainer uses, AppleRawMaxCapacity ÷ DesignCapacity ([batt #124](https://github.com/charlie0129/batt/issues/124)). Qmax measures the cells' chemical capacity with no load attached. Nominal and full-charge capacity measure usable capacity, and they fall faster as internal resistance rises. The 93.2 % nominal figure is the more conservative health reading, and calendar fade explains much of it, since fade concentrates in the first months. The pace matters more than the level. 97 cycles in at most eight months is at least 12 cycles a month, so the 1,000-cycle mark arrives around 2033 at the current rate, and sooner if the machine was bought after launch. **Proposal:** Talon should flag the battery only if raw health keeps dropping faster than about 2 % per 100 cycles after cycle 150.

The SSD numbers are reassuring. Apple publishes no endurance rating. Howard Oakley's 2026 estimate is at least 3,000 erase-write cycles for Apple NAND, "probably 6,000 or more" ([Eclectic Light, Feb 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/26/how-long-will-my-macs-ssd-last/)). That works out to about **1,500 TBW for 512 GB**. One M1 Air reported in comments showed 10 % used after 136 TB written. At about 18 TB/year, even Oakley's cautious 2022 figure of 300 TBW ([Eclectic Light, 2022](https://eclecticlight.co/2022/12/05/how-to-estimate-an-ssds-working-life/)) lasts about 16 years. Two SMART fields mislead on Apple drives. Power-On Hours under-counts badly: one brand-new Mac showed 130 power cycles but 3 hours ([MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/brand-new-mac-from-apple-showing-130-power-cycles-but-only-3-power-on-hours-should-i-be-worried.2445626)). Percentage Used is an integer estimate based on written bytes, and Oakley calls relying on it "a gamble". Unsafe Shutdowns counts power loss without a shutdown notice, which covers panics, forced power-offs and a drained battery ([Netdata](https://www.netdata.cloud/guides/smartctl-disk-monitoring/smartctl-unsafe-shutdowns/)). Oakley called 23 on a Mac Studio "a large number", so 20 here is worth tracking but is not a fault on its own. It matters only if it rises together with panic logs. Oakley also calls swap "the worst enemy" of SSD life, so the small swap use here is good news ([Eclectic Light, Feb 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/26/how-long-will-my-macs-ssd-last/)).

## What Talon should track

Every "Healthy range" and "Alert threshold" below is a **Proposal** unless the Basis column names a source. Sources marked "undocumented" are private Apple keys that can change with any macOS release. Talon should parse `ioreg -a` output as a plist rather than with grep ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255613997)), and should fall back gracefully when a key reads 0. On macOS 27, the top-level `DesignCapacity` and `Temperature` keys read 0.

Display principles follow from this. Show Apple's 97 % as the headline battery figure and the raw 93.2 % as a secondary "gauge" line, so the two do not appear to contradict each other. ## Habits that matter most for this Mac

| Rank | Habit | Why it matters here | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Turn on the built-in Charge Limit (80 %) if the Mac is mostly plugged in | Time at 100 % is the largest controllable cause of calendar ageing; 80 % captures most of the benefit | Sourced: [Apple 102338](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102338), [BU-808](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-808-how-to-prolong-lithium-based-batteries), [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000133836/117458694); exact benefit for this pack is unknown |
| 2 | Keep it out of heat, especially while charging: no hot cars, no charging under blankets or in insulating cases | Ambient above 35 °C permanently reduces capacity; heat at full charge is the worst case | Sourced: [Apple](https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/) |
| 3 | Keep a current backup | A soldered SSD means a drive or board failure is unrecoverable without one | Sourced: [Oakley](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/26/how-long-will-my-macs-ssd-last/); soldering is **Unverified** for M5 |
| 4 | Avoid routinely running down to 0 % | Shallow cycles last much longer than full ones | Sourced: [BU-808](https://batteryuniversity.com/article/bu-808-how-to-prolong-lithium-based-batteries) |
| 5 | Keep at least 60 GB (ideally 15–25 %) free | Helps wear levelling, swap and updates | Sourced: [Oakley](https://eclecticlight.co/2022/11/29/how-much-unified-memory-does-your-apple-silicon-mac-need/) |
| 6 | Quit idle Electron/LLM apps and stale Safari windows when swap climbs | Keeps swap, the main avoidable SSD writer, low on 16 GB | Sourced principle ([Oakley](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/26/how-long-will-my-macs-ssd-last/)); app-specific advice is Proposal |
| 7 | Use a hard surface for long heavy jobs | Fabric blocks the bottom panel, a main heat-spreading surface, so throttling comes sooner and the battery runs warmer | Proposal; no M5-specific measurements |
| 8 | Store at about 50 % if unused for weeks; top up to 50 % every six months | Avoids both deep discharge and full-charge storage | Sourced: [Apple](https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/) |

Some things are not worth the user's attention: die temperatures around 100 °C under sustained load, SSD read volume, the drive's Power-On Hours, unified-log writes (only 250–500 MB/day per [Oakley](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/02/26/how-long-will-my-macs-ssd-last/)), manual battery "calibration" rituals, and RAM-cleaner apps.

## Claims that remain unverified

Several things a builder would want to rely on rest on thin evidence:

- **SSD and RAM soldering on the M5 Air.** Inferred from Apple's history and early press; no teardown confirms it.
- **The bundled charger.** Sources say 30 W or 40 W depending on configuration and region.
- **GPU core count on the user's configuration.** Notebookcheck's entry unit had 8 cores, while Apple says "up to 10". Talon should read it from the IORegistry.
- **How Apple computes "Maximum Capacity".** The match with Qmax is one data point.
- **Meanings of private battery fields.** `NotChargingReason`, `WeightedRa`, `DailyMinSoc`/`DailyMaxSoc` and `PermanentFailureStatus` have no public documentation and must be learned empirically on the machine.
- **Sensor names.** tdie/tdev/tcal/PMU2 semantics and the M5 SMC key list are community reverse-engineering, and no efficiency-core temperature key is known for the base M5.
- **M5 Air die temperatures and pressure levels.** No published die-temperature trace exists, and it is unknown whether the machine reaches "heavy" pressure in practice.
- **Stuck calibration on macOS 27.** The bug that overrides charge limits is documented on other Macs, but only anecdotally on an M5 Air.
- **SSD endurance figures.** They extrapolate from M1-era and older Apple drives.
- **macOS 27 paths and tools.** The log locations and the `tmutil`/`sfltool` behaviour Talon would rely on have not been re-checked on macOS 27, and Oakley notes macOS 27 already changed Trim log entries ([Eclectic Light, Sep 2026](https://eclecticlight.co/2026/09/23/checking-an-ssds-trims-in-golden-gate/)).

No recall or widespread hardware defect for the M5 Air was found in sources through late 2026.

## Conclusion

On this machine, longevity is mostly decided by the battery, and the battery is decided by habits rather than workload. The SSD is on track to outlast the machine's useful life by a decade or more. The chip runs hot by design and throttles politely. Memory pressure is modest. The real signal in this snapshot is that the battery's 93.2 % nominal capacity runs ahead of Apple's reassuring 97 %. Whether that gap is front-loaded calendar fade or the start of above-spec wear will only show in the trend over the next 50–100 cycles, which is exactly what Talon is positioned to capture.

That changes the brief for Talon. Its value is not in reproducing the dashboards that iStat Menus or DriveDx already offer. It is in three things they lack: a longitudinal battery record that reconciles Apple's figure with the raw gauge, an honest explanation of throttling keyed to pressure levels rather than scary temperatures, and a check that the Charge Limit is actually being honoured on a macOS release where limits have been seen to fail. Because so many of the inputs are undocumented private keys, Talon should store raw values verbatim, re-validate them after every macOS update, and keep its alert rules versioned so the thresholds can be retuned as this machine's own history accumulates.
